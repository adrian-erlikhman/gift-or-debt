    §02 · How it works

    Name every dollar. Then do the math in the open.

    1. 01

      Read the letter

      Rules find every dollar amount and what it is: cost, grant, loan or work-study. Totals are set aside and checked against the lines. Fall/spring columns and “per semester” amounts become a year. Every line keeps the words it came from, and you can fix any of them.

    2. 02

      Split the money

      Net price is the cost of attendance minus gift aid. Loans and work-study don't lower it; they're ways to pay it. Over four years costs grow, first-year-only awards drop off, borrowing repeats and interest builds on unsubsidized, private and Parent PLUS loans while you're in school.

    3. 03

      Flag it, then ask

      Loans counted as aid. A Parent PLUS loan closing the gap. Books and travel left out. A scholarship that's first-year only. Totals that don't match their lines. Then it drafts polite, specific questions for the aid office.

    §03 · Where Claude fits

    Claude can read the letter. It never does the math.

    Without a key, everything runs on rules and plain arithmetic. With your own Anthropic key, held only in this tab, Claude reads what the rules can't: a photo of a paper letter, a scanned PDF, an odd layout. It can also reword the email.

    • Every amount is checked. Before the page uses a line Claude read, its dollar figure has to appear in the letter's text. A number the model invents or misreads is dropped.
    • Photos are marked. Lines read from a photo can't be checked that way, so each one is flagged for you to confirm.
    • The email keeps its numbers. Every dollar figure in Claude's rewording has to appear in the draft, or the draft is kept.
    • The math is code. Every number on the page comes from js/money.js, covered by tests.

    Loan terms are for loans first disbursed July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 (Federal Student Aid, GENERAL-26-33): 6.52% for undergraduate Direct Loans with a 1.057% fee, 9.07% for Parent PLUS with a 4.228% fee. Monthly payments assume the standard 10-year plan. Every assumption can be changed on the page. These are estimates to check with the school, not financial advice.
